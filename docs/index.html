Eight Channels, One Vector

A survey of retrieval-augmented visual document understanding

— papers · — from 2025–26 · GitHub · + add a paper

Surveys of this literature organise it by where computation happens — parsing, retrieval, generation. This one organises it by what the page contains: eight content channels that co-occur, and overlap, on the same page.

Two of those eight have no retrieval literature at all. That is the finding, and it is visible below before you read a word of the paper.

Abstract

Document collections have outgrown what a model can hold in context, so retrieval is now a precondition for document understanding rather than an optimisation. Existing surveys organise this literature by pipeline stage or by retrieval modality: by where computation happens. We organise it by what the page contains. A page carries up to eight kinds of content at once — plain text, layout, tables, figures, equations, form fields, stamps and seals, and typography — and these overlap spatially rather than partitioning the page. Because they overlap, the pooling that produces a page representation is not injective, and we formalise the resulting channel interference as a second-order interaction between co-located channels.

Three things follow. We build a channel × paradigm matrix whose empty cells identify directions on which nothing has been reported. Analysing the published record rather than re-running systems, we find that independent reports of the same method, benchmark and metric differ by a median that bounds what any single reported improvement can be taken to mean. We introduce a channel-aware retrieval score and an 18-item reporting checklist, and measure how far current practice meets it.

We screened 1,778 records across the venues listed in the paper and cite 286. The screened pool, the result record and the checklist are released here.
